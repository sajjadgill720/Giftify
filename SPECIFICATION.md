# Pakistan Personalized Art & Gifting Platform — Build and Business Plan

Research date: 6 October 2026, Asia/Karachi  
Prepared for: Sajjad Ur Rehman  
Status: research-informed plan; no supplier, customer, payment-provider, or legal agreements have been obtained.  
Currency: PKR unless explicitly stated. “Millions” is interpreted as millions of PKR per month, not USD.

## 1. The decision

Build a personalized-art brand with two connected products:

1. **Consumer storefront:** meaningful maps, milestone art, voice-memory prints, and selected illustration products.
2. **Business gifting service:** a paid recurring service that helps employers plan, approve, personalize, fund, and fulfill gifts throughout the year.

Use the same templates, rendering engine, suppliers, payment ledger, and fulfillment operations. Do not build two separate platforms.

**The recurring-revenue thesis is a hypothesis:** employers may pay for reliable ongoing coordination, budget control, and fulfillment. A poster shop does not become a subscription business merely because customers sometimes reorder. The proposed business is initially a software-enabled managed service, not high-margin pure SaaS.

Start with a founder-operated pilot around Islamabad/Rawalpindi. Sell to organizations with an existing gifting budget and a named HR/operations owner. Expand fulfillment and sales to Lahore and Karachi after quality is repeatable.

### Commercial targets, not promises

| Target | Required arithmetic | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rs. 1 million monthly product revenue | 251 orders at Rs. 3,990 = Rs. 1,001,490 | Recurrence, profitability, or affordable acquisition |
| Rs. 1 million service MRR | 40 active contracts at Rs. 25,000/month | That employers will accept the fee |
| Rs. 2 million service MRR | 80 active contracts at Rs. 25,000/month | Capacity, retention, or positive cash flow |
| Rs. 5 million service MRR | 200 active contracts at Rs. 25,000/month | That a solo founder can operate it |

Priority: demonstrate three paid business pilots, then two genuine renewals, before investing heavily in subscription automation. Consumer sales should validate artwork, packaging, and production, not subsidize unlimited corporate customization.

## 2. Evidence standards and research limits

This document separates:

- **Observed:** a public page, price, policy, or capability explicitly described by a source.
- **Inference:** a conclusion drawn from those observations.
- **Assumption:** a planning number or proposed feature requiring validation.
- **Gate:** evidence required before spending or expanding.

Sources are linked in Section 23. Prices were reviewed during this research, but may depend on variant, quantity, taxes, location, or cached page content. Vendor marketing claims are not independently audited. No test purchases, interviews, private dashboards, supplier visits, or live payment integrations were performed.

There is no credible, sufficiently narrow market-size estimate here for Pakistani personalized wall art or paid gifting-management subscriptions. Do not invent a TAM by multiplying the whole population by a poster price. Build a prospect list and measured conversion funnel instead.

### What the evidence supports

- Buyers can already obtain personalized frames, corporate gifts, and scheduled rewards.
- The opportunity is execution for a narrow customer segment, not a claim of no competition.
- An employer may prefer vouchers, useful merchandise, or cash to another framed object. Offer milestone artwork selectively and test recipient preferences.
- Public prices span different materials and sizes. Competing against a low-cost glassless frame with a premium acrylic-front product is not a like-for-like comparison.

### Proposed advantage to test

For employers with small recurring batches: one place to maintain a calendar, apply a budget, approve exact artwork, coordinate individual deliveries, and reconcile spending. Distinctive bilingual artwork is the creative component; fewer missed occasions and less coordination work are the business outcome.

Competitors may provide these privately. Ask prospects what their current vendor already does before claiming a feature gap.

### Demand and payment context

SBP’s Q1 FY26 review reports 2.8 billion retail transactions and a 90% digital share by volume for July–September 2025 [S11]. These figures describe broad payments, not the share of e-commerce orders prepaid, and do not establish demand for this product. A newer quarterly-report index exists [S12]; the older numeric baseline is used only as context, not as the latest quarter.

Use prepaid business orders and payment links where possible. Keep assisted ordering available. Do not infer that consumer COD is unnecessary from economy-wide digital-payment statistics.

## 4. Customer focus and positioning

### Primary recurring customer

An employer with approximately 50–500 employees, an identifiable HR/People Ops/administration owner, recurring recognition activity, and willingness to fund orders in advance. Begin with software firms, agencies, professional-services businesses, and distributed teams that can be reached through warm introductions.

Qualification questions:

1. What did you spend on employee/client gifting in the last 12 months?
2. How many orders and recipients were involved?
3. Who coordinates names, artwork, approvals, delivery, and invoices?
4. What failed recently, and what did that cost in time or money?
5. Is the buyer authorized to pay an ongoing management fee?
6. Will staff actually value artwork? Which occasions justify it?
7. Can you prepay gifts, or do procurement rules require credit?

Disqualify organizations buying only one annual Eid hamper, those with no budget owner, and those requiring long unsecured credit at pilot stage. Large headcount alone does not imply fit.

### Secondary consumers

- Couples and family gift buyers: anniversaries, new homes, parents, newborns.
- Overseas Pakistanis paying for delivery to recipients inside Pakistan.
- Graduates/alumni purchasing campus and achievement memories.

Do not launch international physical shipping in the MVP. Overseas payment acceptance and domestic fulfillment are separate capabilities.

### Positioning

Consumer proposition: **Personal artwork for the places, people, and moments that matter.**

Business proposition: **Plan and deliver personal milestone gifts with controlled budgets, approved designs, and one accountable team.**

Promise only what operations can measure. Avoid guaranteed employee retention, guaranteed delivery dates without courier support, or “lifetime” digital hosting.

### Launch limits

- Two exact print sizes: A4 (210 × 297 mm) and A3 (297 × 420 mm).
- Two frame finishes; define actual material, front protection, backing, and outer dimensions.
- Six to nine original templates total, not hundreds.
- Urdu and English text tested with actual fonts and mixed-direction content.
- One proof revision included; additional illustration work priced explicitly.
- No generic copyrighted anime/film-art catalog, unrestricted image-generation playground, or marketplace of unvetted suppliers.

### Making corporate gifts repeatable without endless frames

Use framed art for meaningful milestones, desk-sized artwork for smaller recognitions, and printed personal cards for frequent occasions. Pilot an opt-out or choice mechanism. Do not send the same employee a large framed print every month. Broader gift options should be added only after actual client demand and supplier validation.

### Memory-link service

Initial offer: a stated three-year hosting period, capped audio length/file size, download/export, explicit content ownership, deletion, and a clear end-of-service policy. Allocate a per-order hosting/support reserve before pricing. Renewal after three years is optional future revenue, not current MRR. No public indexing of family pages. An unguessable QR link is a bearer link, not strong authentication; offer PIN/access controls when needed.

## 7. Recurring model, pricing, and honest revenue reporting

### Proposed business tiers — test prices, not established market rates

| Tier | Monthly fee, excluding applicable tax | Scope ceiling | Included coordination | Goods |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Essential | 10,000 | Up to 50 active recipients, one admin | Up to 10 gift orders/month, one brand kit, calendar and proofs | Charged separately |
| Growth | 25,000 | Up to 250 active recipients, three admins | Up to 30 gift orders/month, approval roles, budget report, monthly review | Charged separately |
| Managed | 50,000 | Up to 750 active recipients, five admins | Up to 80 gift orders/month, multiple teams and service coordinator | Charged separately |

Coordination limits are service capacity, not free physical gifts or guaranteed consumption. Extra orders incur a disclosed coordination fee or tier upgrade after an actual time study. No unlimited custom artwork. Quote optional setup at Rs. 15,000–30,000 for data cleanup/brand templates; exclude setup fees from MRR.

Start with a 30-day paid pilot. Offer a subsequent three-month agreement with explicit cancellation/renewal terms, not an assumed perpetual commitment. Invoice the fee monthly in advance; pre-fund physical orders. Start with invoicing/payment links; automate mandates only after approval and testing.

### Willingness-to-pay test

A Growth customer sending 20 gifts at Rs. 3,000 each pays Rs. 85,000/month before applicable tax: Rs. 25,000 service + Rs. 60,000 goods. Effective cost is Rs. 4,250 per gift. With only five gifts, effective cost becomes Rs. 8,000. Low-volume customers may rationally reject this.

Ask the buyer to compare that with their current supplier bill plus documented administrative effort and failure cost. Measure hours before/after; do not invent savings. If employers value the goods but reject the separate fee, sell profitable managed orders, reduce scope, or change the segment. Do not relabel a physical-product bundle as SaaS.

### What counts as recurring

| Metric | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Service MRR | Normalized monthly value of active recurring service contracts, net of discounts and cancellations; show delinquent accounts separately |
| Annual fee collected upfront | Cash now, monthly service revenue as earned; normalize net annual contract value over 12 months |
| Repeat gift sales | Product revenue when earned, not MRR merely because purchases repeat |
| Committed physical subscription | Separate physical-subscription metric and fulfillment obligation; do not blend with software/service MRR |
| Customer prepayment for future gifts | Customer advance/liability until fulfilled, not immediate revenue |
| Gift cards / unspent credits | Outstanding obligations; obtain appropriate terms/accounting advice |
| Setup and custom-design charges | Nonrecurring revenue |
| GMV in a future marketplace | Not automatically your revenue; determine principal-versus-agent treatment |

No transferable stored-value wallet in the MVP. Use allocated customer advances against invoices/orders. Do not offer interest, peer transfers, or cash-out features.

### Alternative recurring paths

1. Photographer/reseller workspace subscription: test only after at least five partners repeatedly use the editor and ask for it. Proposed fee Rs. 5,000–15,000/month, with goods separate.
2. Family annual memory-book/art plan: test later; physical fulfillment is substantial and retention is unproven.
3. Digital memory storage: a modest add-on, not the principal route to millions.

Do not build all three alongside employer gifting.

## 8. Market validation and go-to-market

### First 30 days

- Interview 20 employer buyers and 10 consumer buyers. Ask about past purchases before pitching.
- Build a manually verified list of 100 relevant organizations, recording headcount band, buyer role, source, current vendor, and next action. This is a prospecting target, not a market census.
- Produce 12 physical samples across two suppliers; send trial parcels to at least three cities.
- Seek three paid corporate pilots and 20 paid consumer orders, including buyers outside friends/family.
- Present Essential and Growth fees explicitly. Record fee acceptance separately from gift purchases.
- Budget Rs. 40,000–70,000 for this manual validation; release additional funds only on evidence.

### Founder-led sales motion

Warm introduction or relevant business outreach; short discovery; sample/demo; written scope and price; paid pilot; monthly review; renewal. No mass unsolicited WhatsApp blasting or uploading employee lists before agreement.

Proposed experiment: 100 well-qualified accounts → 20 substantive responses → 8 meetings → 3 paid pilots → 2 retained accounts. These are assumptions to measure. If actual responses or renewals are lower, revise the plan rather than increasing forecast certainty.

### Acquisition channels

| Channel | Experiment | Success measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employer direct sales | Sample-led outreach to 100 qualified organizations | Paid fee, sales cycle, acquisition cost, renewal |
| Photographer partnerships | Five studios, small sample kit, tracked referral terms | Delivered orders and contribution after commission |
| University/alumni | One graduation-themed pilot | Paid demand without institution-logo misuse |
| Creator content | Three relevant creators with capped sample/commission budgets | Delivered-order CAC, not views |
| Consumer advertising | Small category-specific campaigns | Contribution after CAC and failed deliveries |
| Search content | Occasion pages with real products and delivery information | Qualified traffic and paid conversion |

Affiliate commissions and sample costs belong in acquisition spending. Do not subtract both a blended CAC and the same commission again. Cap ads daily and pause a category when the economics fail after sufficient purchases to understand the signal.

### Retention value

Show the next 30 days of approved occasions, spending against budget, fulfillment exceptions, and a monthly reconciliation. Track time saved only from a real baseline. Ask recipients whether the item is useful/desirable. Customer data export and clear cancellation build trust; lock-in is not the product.

All proposed prices below are planning revenue before applicable sales/service taxes. Do not show them as final checkout totals until tax treatment is confirmed. Cost estimates include operational allowances but are not supplier quotations. Income taxes and withholding effects are not resolved here.

The Rs. 1,100 production target is below the cited Rs. 1,450 retail frame benchmark, and therefore requires an actual negotiated specification/quote. A premium upgrade cannot silently inherit cheaper materials.

### Sensitivities

| Changed assumption | Contribution/order, before fixed costs/taxes |
| --- | ---: |
| Base | 1,090 |

Custom illustrations require extra artist/revision costs. Audio products need a long-term hosting/support allocation. Neither should use the base template margin unchanged.

These are pre-tax economic break-even figures, with no arbitrary tax reserve. They differ from the earlier chat model that added a provisional per-order reserve.

### Recurring service unit economics

| Tier | Fee | Direct account-service cost assumption | Contribution |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Essential | 10,000 | 3,000 | 7,000 |
| Growth | 25,000 | 8,000 | 17,000 |
| Managed | 50,000 | 20,000 | 30,000 |

Direct service costs cover allocated coordinator time, brand/approval support, account-level software, and collection-fee allowance. Time-track them. Per-gift physical handling remains in the separate gift cost. Shared product development and general administration remain fixed overhead. Never count the same salary in all three places.

Growth account example: acquisition cost Rs. 25,000 / monthly service contribution Rs. 17,000 = **1.47 months payback after revenue starts**, excluding sales-cycle delay. Both inputs are hypotheses. At CAC Rs. 60,000 and direct account cost Rs. 15,000, payback becomes six months. Do not use a speculative lifetime value to justify losses before retention data exists.

### COD and cash flow

With 10% dispatch failure and Rs. 2,000 unrecoverable cost per failed order, allocate (0.10 / 0.90) × 2,000 = **Rs. 222.22 per delivered order**. Recompute delivered-order CAC as well. Do not charge prepaid-card fees to COD orders unless actually incurred; model COD collection fees and deductions separately.

For consumer custom work, test an explicitly disclosed production deposit and verify the remaining collection method. For employers, pre-fund production until credit underwriting is justified. Payment screenshots are supporting evidence, not confirmed settlement.

At 400 corporate gifts/month with Rs. 2,150 direct cost, one month of production outflow is **Rs. 860,000**. If customers pay 30 days later and suppliers require upfront payment, that working-capital gap can overwhelm a profitable business. Deposits and approved credit limits matter more than cosmetic dashboard features.

## 10. Path to millions: scenarios and capacity

### Example at 60 recurring business customers

| Revenue/cost | Monthly PKR |
| --- | ---: |
| 20 Essential × 10,000 | 200,000 |
| 30 Growth × 25,000 | 750,000 |
| 10 Managed × 50,000 | 500,000 |
| **Service MRR** | **1,450,000** |
| Direct service costs: 20×3,000 + 30×8,000 + 10×20,000 | (500,000) |
| **Service contribution** | **950,000** |
| Physical revenue: 400 gifts × 3,000 | 1,200,000 |
| Physical direct costs: 400 × 2,150 | (860,000) |
| **Physical contribution** | **340,000** |
| Shared overhead and acquisition budget | (600,000) |
| **Illustrative operating profit before tax** | **690,000** |

Total monthly revenue is Rs. 2.65 million; only Rs. 1.45 million is service MRR. The 400 gifts are a separate demand assumption, not included free gifts and not automatically recurring revenue.

Shared Rs. 600,000 overhead scenario: engineering/product Rs. 250,000; founder/general management Rs. 100,000; acquisition/sales Rs. 120,000; content/brand Rs. 80,000; general administration Rs. 50,000. Direct account coordinators are funded from the Rs. 500,000 direct-service line. Physical production labor is funded from gift costs. These are role budgets, not verified salary benchmarks.

Downside at the same customer count: 20% lower realized fees reduce service contribution by Rs. 290,000 if costs remain fixed; gifts falling to 200 reduce contribution by Rs. 170,000; overhead rising to Rs. 800,000 reduces profit by another Rs. 200,000. Combined operating profit becomes **Rs. 30,000 before tax**. The million-rupee revenue headline hides substantial execution risk.

### Acquisition/retention illustration

After validation, assume six new paying accounts per month, monthly logo churn of 2.5%, and constant average fee Rs. 25,000. Starting from zero:

`accounts[n] = accounts[n-1] × 0.975 + 6`

| Months after scaled sales begins | Expected accounts, fractional planning value | Approximate service MRR |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 3 | 17.55 | 438,844 |
| 6 | 33.82 | 845,590 |
| 12 | 62.88 | 1,572,010 |
| 18 | 87.84 | 2,196,054 |
| 24 | 109.29 | 2,732,151 |

This is a sensitivity model, not a timeline promise. It excludes expansion, discounts, collection failures, sales ramp, and tax. At a 2% retained-account conversion assumption, six new accounts require approximately 300 qualified target accounts/month. At 60 accounts, 2.5% churn loses 1.5 accounts/month on average. A one-off campaign cannot sustain this.

With only two new accounts/month and 2.5% churn, the long-run equilibrium is 80 accounts, approached gradually. Scaling acquisition and maintaining retention are essential for the Rs. 2–5 million MRR goal.

### Operational capacity

400 gifts across 22 working days is about 18 gifts/day, with spikes around occasions. Validate suppliers for at least twice normal daily load before selling date-sensitive campaigns. At 60 accounts, three coordination hours/account/month already require 180 hours before exception handling. Staff from measured hours, not account counts alone.

No fixed 12-month promise for millions. A credible roadmap is milestone-driven: paid pilots, renewals, 10 accounts, 25 accounts, then 40–80 retained accounts with staffing and cash controls.

## 12. Production, suppliers, and service standards

### Procurement specification

Obtain written quotes from at least two suppliers for quantities 1, 10, 25, 50, and 100. Each quote must identify print area, outer frame size, paper brand/weight/finish, printing process, frame material, protective front, hanging hardware, packaging, lead time, taxes, freight, and remake responsibility. Confirm whether each unit may have different artwork at that price.

Sample acceptance:

- Exact names, spelling, alignment, and clean Urdu joins.
- Actual A4/A3 artwork area, correct cropping and no content under the frame lip.
- Color and black-line consistency across repeated samples.
- No scratches, loose backs, sharp edges, or inadequate hanging fixtures.
- Printed QR scans on two phone types under ordinary indoor light.
- Packed-parcel measurement, weight, and real delivery test.

Leopards publishes a volumetric rule for certain services using length × breadth × height in cm / 5,000 [S17]. A 50 × 40 × 6 cm box is 2.4 kg volumetric weight. Confirm chargeable weight, route, taxes, COD terms, return charges, fragile-item exclusions, liability caps, and settlement schedule in the actual courier agreement. No generic 500 g rate is an A3 shipping quote.

### Operational promise

During pilots, display an estimated dispatch window only after supplier confirmation. Use a ten-working-day planning lead for scheduled occasions initially, reducing it only when real performance supports it. Distinguish order date, proof approval, dispatch estimate, and delivery estimate.

Late customer approval or address confirmation moves the date; the system must flag the change before payment/production. Offer an optional digital greeting on the occasion date when the buyer explicitly accepts it; it is not a substitute for a promised physical delivery.

### Daily operations queue

Payment awaiting reconciliation; proof awaiting approval; ready for production; supplier acknowledgement overdue; quality failure; dispatch due; courier exception; delivered awaiting closure; replacement/refund due. Each item has owner, deadline, and audit record.

Use approved substitute materials only after customer approval when the visible specification changes. A supplier cannot mark an order delivered. The courier's delivered event does not prove customer satisfaction; keep a dispute path.

## 13. Product scope and user journeys

### Roles

| Role | Allowed actions |
| --- | --- |
| Consumer / guest purchaser | Design, quote, pay, approve their proof, track their order, request support |
| Business owner | Manage organization, plan, users, budget, recipients, approvals, invoices |
| Business editor | Prepare recipient lists and designs; cannot spend unless separately authorized |
| Business approver | Approve assigned campaigns within granted budget limits |
| Recipient | Confirm permitted delivery details or choose among allowed options; cannot see employer budgets/other recipients |
| Operations staff | Access assigned production/support work, release eligible jobs, resolve exceptions |
| Supplier | Only assigned production files and necessary fulfillment data; no client financial history |
| Platform administrator | Audited exceptional actions; not an everyday shared login |

### Consumer journey

Browse occasion → select template/SKU → personalize → preview → server quote → checkout → verified payment/deposit → final proof if required → approve locked version → production → QA → shipping → delivery/support.

If proof changes price, require a new quote and settlement of the difference. If payment succeeds but render fails, hold the order and contact the buyer; do not silently print a fallback design.

### Business journey

Sign paid pilot → organization and brand setup → validate recipient CSV → choose upcoming occasions → select approved products/budget → generate draft campaign → approver reviews itemized proof and total → pre-fund invoice → operations release → shipping exceptions → reconciliation → renewal.

Start with a human approval for every campaign. Autopilot is later, opt-in, and bounded by monetary limits, allowed products, and current recipient data.

## 14. Technical architecture

### Chosen default

Use a modular TypeScript application with a separate rendering/background worker, not microservices. Default stack: Next.js/React, TypeScript, PostgreSQL through Supabase, Supabase Auth and private object storage, and a containerized Node worker. Next.js documentation supports the selected application framework; select and pin supported versions at implementation time [S18]. If an existing team codebase already standardizes on TanStack Start, document one substitution before scaffolding rather than mixing frameworks.

| Component | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Web application | Storefront, business portal, operations console, authenticated API |
| Postgres | Transactions, tenant data, pricing, workflow state, billing ledger, job queue/outbox |
| Private object storage | Originals, proof images, print files, audio, delivery evidence |
| Worker | Deterministic renders, PDF export, media processing, scheduled jobs, provider reconciliation |
| Hosted payment provider | Checkout/payment links, approved recurring billing capability |
| Supplier/courier adapters | Manual export first, authenticated integrations only after validation |
| Monitoring | Exceptions, job failures, settlement mismatches, spend alerts, audit trail |

Deploy web and worker separately so CPU-heavy rendering does not block checkout. Initially use a durable Postgres job table with leases, retry metadata, and unique business keys. Add a specialized queue only when measured throughput/operational needs justify it. ## 15. Rendering, map data, and print fidelity

### Deterministic template engine

Template schema contains physical dimensions, bleed, safe area, permitted text fields, language/font constraints, palette, image bounds, map bounds, and optional waveform/QR placement. Preview and print use the same versioned design representation. Do not generate a print file from a browser screenshot.

Render text with tested font shaping and embedding. Keep fonts local/licensed where required. Test Urdu ligatures, right-to-left text with Latin names/numbers, diacritics, and long names. Reject overflow or ask the user to choose a shorter layout; do not silently shrink text to illegibility.

A3 at 300 pixels/inch is approximately 3,508 × 4,961 pixels before bleed. Prefer vector text and lines in the final PDF, with sufficient raster resolution for images. Obtain the printer's actual bleed and color-profile requirements; an RGB preview is not a guaranteed print-color match. Store the final approved PDF checksum in the production job.

### Maps

Use MapLibre GL JS for interactive map presentation if selected after a render prototype [S19]. Map data, geocoding, fonts, styles, and tiles each have separate licensing/usage terms.

OpenStreetMap data requires attribution and appropriate licence handling [S20]. Its public raster tile servers prohibit bulk downloading and do not provide a production SLA [S21]. Choose a provider explicitly permitting commercial print/static export or use licensed data rendered by your own service. Do not use public OSM tiles as an unrestricted print backend. Do not scrape Google Maps for commercial artwork.

Confirm required attribution on the actual printed product, not only the website footer. Do not assert that all artwork must be open-sourced; database and produced-work obligations need the correct licence interpretation.

### AI-assisted images

Treat AI output as an optional visual asset with human approval. Keep names, exact dates, maps, Urdu text, logos, and QR codes in deterministic layers. Record model/provider, generation settings, output version, and commercial-use permissions. Limit attempts and revisions. Customer photographs are not marketing/training assets by default.

### Audio/QR

Generate waveform from the actual audio samples, not an invented decorative wave. Render the QR from a stable application URL with revocable token. Test printed quiet zones and contrast. Cap audio duration, strip unnecessary metadata, and isolate media processing. Deleting audio must revoke playback and explain that an already printed QR can no longer provide it.

## 16. Workflow correctness and billing

### Separate state machines

Do not collapse all states into one `status` column.

- Payment: pending, authorized where supported, captured/settled as defined by provider, partially refunded, refunded, disputed.
- Design: draft, preview-ready, approval-pending, approved, superseded.
- Production: blocked, queued, supplier-accepted, printing, QA, ready, failed/rework.
- Fulfillment: unbooked, booked, collected, in-transit, delivered, exception, returned.
- Subscription: trial only if explicitly offered, active, past-due, cancel-at-period-end, canceled.

Production release requires a verified qualifying payment/customer advance, exact approved design version, available product specification, and authorized release. A successful browser redirect is not proof of payment.

### Payment rules

Safepay advertises card, wallet/Raast, and subscription capabilities [S13]. Its published service agreement describes subscription administration and failed-payment behavior [S22]. Actual merchant approval, supported recurring instruments, fees, settlements, and current integration contracts must be confirmed in sandbox and onboarding. Do not assume a one-time Raast/wallet payment creates a recurring mandate.

Use provider-verified webhooks and reconciliation. Check amount, currency, merchant/order reference, event uniqueness, and valid state transitions. A duplicate or delayed webhook must never produce a second order or shipment. A timed-out checkout may still have succeeded; query/reconcile before offering an unsafe retry.

Manual transfer reconciliation requires matching a real bank/payment record to an invoice. Store who verified it and when. No automatic release from an uploaded screenshot.

### Recurring billing rules

- Store explicit recurring consent and the accepted price/plan version.
- Separate service-fee invoice from gift purchases, even if presented together.
- Annual discounts reduce normalized MRR; do not count list price.
- Handle provider cancellations/dunning as real state changes; do not create competing blind charge retries.
- After failed renewal, pause new service actions under disclosed terms; honor already funded physical orders.
- Cancellation stops future service renewals at the agreed boundary. Refund/complete outstanding goods separately.
- New price versions apply through the agreed notice/consent process, never by mutating old invoices.

### Scheduling and budgets

Generate future occasion instances idempotently. Explicitly define February 29 policy, weekend/holiday handling, approval deadlines, archive/offboarding behavior, and organization timezone. An employee removed before the funding/production cutoff must not receive an automatically generated new order.

Budget reservation must be atomic: two approvers cannot spend the same available advance. Use transaction locks/constraints and unique campaign release keys. Expired/canceled orders release reservations exactly once. Reconcile advance liabilities to bank/provider settlement and open funded orders daily.

### Job reliability

Every side effect uses a stable idempotency key; every retry is bounded. A worker lease expires after crash and can be reclaimed. Supplier submission timeouts require lookup/reconciliation before resubmission. If the supplier lacks idempotency support, hold ambiguous jobs for operator review. Log references, not raw photos/audio/recipient CSVs.

## 17. Security, privacy, and applicable compliance work

### Access boundaries

Supabase documentation distinguishes object grants from row-level policies and warns that privileged server credentials bypass ordinary row restrictions [S23]. Use least-privilege grants and tested row-level policies for exposed tables/storage. Never expose service credentials in the client. Server-side privileged jobs must still verify organization ownership and record their purpose.

Uploads require actual MIME validation, size/duration limits, malware checks as appropriate, sanitized filenames, private storage, and signed downloads. Block arbitrary user-supplied remote URLs in renderers to prevent internal-network access. Sanitize/flatten SVG; disable embedded scripts, external resources, and unsafe PDF content. Rate-limit generation and authentication.

### Recipient and child information

Collect only what the workflow needs. Birthday reminders usually need month/day, not birth year. Avoid CNIC, salary, medical details, or unrelated HR records. Limit suppliers to the fields needed for production/delivery; delete transient exports after the agreed window.

For family/child uploads, require the uploader's appropriate authority and consent; do not publish child images or use them in advertising without separate permission. Set original-upload, print-file, accounting-record, and memory-page retention separately. A proposed default is 90 days for nonessential raw uploads, subject to customer reprint choice and applicable obligations; this is a product policy proposal, not a statutory period.

### Pakistan setup and tax gate

SECP describes single-member company incorporation and its digital filing process [S24]. Ask a Pakistani accountant/legal adviser to choose the appropriate operating structure and establish registration, business banking, invoicing, gateway onboarding, and contracts. Do not imply incorporation is the only way to test demand or that it alone completes tax registration.

FBR's index lists the tax-year 2027 rate card updated under Finance Act 2026 [S25]. The linked current PDF could not be reliably retrieved during this research. The historical 2025 circular describes 1% digital/2% COD withholding, but this plan does **not** certify those as your current complete liability [S26]. Federal goods tax, service tax jurisdiction, seller classification, registration, exemptions, adjustable/final deductions, and corporate procurement withholding require current confirmation.

Before taking live payments, complete a signed-off tax matrix for physical goods, digital artwork, management/service fees, prepaid advances, refunds, COD, and overseas-paid/domestically-delivered orders. Store tax type, rate, basis, effective dates, and creditability separately. Withholding may affect settlement cash without being an additional final expense; do not blindly subtract every deduction twice.

Advertise total payable prices clearly once configured. No arbitrary 5% reserve should be described as tax compliance. Source and font licences, map attribution, original artwork rights, returns/replacement terms, recurring consent, and data handling belong in the launch checklist because they directly affect this product.

## 18. Testing and measurable launch acceptance

Prioritize tests that protect money, personal data, correct artwork, and physical fulfillment.

| Test | Acceptance criterion |
| --- | --- |
| Tenant isolation | Organization A cannot read/write/export B's recipients, assets, invoices, or signed media URLs |
| Guest ownership | Random/expired tokens cannot expose orders; claimed orders remain correctly owned |
| Price tampering | Client-edited totals never change server charge; expired quote requires requote |
| Duplicate payment event | Ten repeats cause one financial posting and one eligible production release |
| Event ordering | Late pending/failed events cannot overwrite verified payment incorrectly |
| Concurrent approvals | Two releases cannot overdraw available customer advance |
| Proof integrity | Printed-file hash is bound to approved design; edits invalidate prior approval |
| Urdu and layout | Reference cases render correctly in preview and PDF with no clipped text |
| Print dimensions | A4/A3, bleed, safe zones, fonts, image resolution checked on real prints |
| QR | Real printed code scans on at least two devices and respects access restrictions |
| Worker crash | Lease recovery completes work without duplicate supplier order |
| Subscription cancellation | No new charge/release after cancellation boundary; funded orders remain tracked |
| Refund | Partial/full refunds reconcile provider, ledger, invoice and customer advance |
| Recipient archive | Future uncommitted occasion orders stop, with clear treatment of committed ones |
| Calendar edge cases | Leap day, timezone conversion, cutoff, holiday adjustment and duplicate scheduler run |
| Upload attack | Oversized/mismatched files, active SVG, external-resource fetches are rejected |
| Backup restore | Database and required object files can be restored together in a staging drill |
| End-to-end operation | Test order goes through payment verification, approval, production, tracking and refund exception |

Pilot technical targets: common template preview under five seconds p95 when assets are ready; ordinary API reads under 750 ms p95 excluding external services; 99% successful non-AI render jobs; alert within five minutes for paid orders stuck without a next step. These are engineering goals to measure, not advertised SLAs.

Initial recovery objectives: at most 24 hours of data loss and restoration within eight hours, backed by independent daily database backups and an object-storage backup strategy. Payment/supplier reconciliation must recover external transactions newer than a backup. Tighten objectives before larger contracts; do not claim these targets until a restore drill passes.

## 20. Metrics, experiments, and stop rules

### Weekly operating dashboard

| Metric | Definition / decision |
| --- | --- |
| Collected service MRR | Active recurring fees normalized monthly; show overdue/uncollected amounts alongside |
| Contracted service MRR | Separate from collected fees; display discounts, cancellations and effective dates |
| Product revenue / contribution | Fulfilled product revenue less direct fulfillment and allocated acquisition |
| Gross revenue retention | Starting recurring revenue less churn/contraction, divided by starting revenue |
| Net revenue retention | Add expansion to the GRR numerator; exclude new customers |
| Logo churn | Customers lost / customers active at period start; small cohorts shown as counts |
| Paid-pilot renewal | Renewed paying organizations / pilots eligible to renew |
| Delivered-order CAC | Attributable acquisition spend / first delivered paid orders |
| Business CAC | Sales, samples and acquisition costs / new paying organizations, with cohort and sales-cycle lag |
| On-time dispatch | Dispatched within the promised dispatch window / dispatches due |
| On-time delivery | Delivered by agreed date / deliveries due; separate courier and internal causes |
| Remake/refund rate | Affected fulfilled orders and actual cost, not just ticket counts |
| Service time | Coordinator hours/account/month; fulfillment minutes/gift separately |
| Cash exposure | Customer advances, payable supplier bills, refunds due, receivables, settlement delays |

Avoid reporting clicks, generated previews, or unsigned letters of interest as revenue validation.

### Pilot gates

- Three businesses pay a separately visible recurring-service fee.
- At least two renew after experiencing the actual service; do not count a free extension.
- Ten-account cohort later demonstrates at least 80% retention over its first 90 days; report exact counts because the sample is small.
- Consumer contribution around Rs. 1,000/order at acceptable delivered-order CAC, or revise pricing/specification.
- No unresolved critical payment, ownership, or print-version defects.
- At least 95% dispatch within the promised window during a meaningful observed period; report the sample size.
- Actual remake costs fit the allowance; otherwise fix production before expanding ads.

These are management thresholds, not universal industry benchmarks.

### Stop/change conditions

| Evidence | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Buyers like gifts but reject service fees in repeated paid offers | Operate as profitable gifting/commerce; stop claiming a subscription thesis |
| Recipient feedback rejects recurring artwork | Offer rarer milestone art; test useful alternatives before broadening catalog |
| Acquisition exceeds margin for two measured cohorts | Stop scaling that channel; change offer, audience, or price |
| Supplier error remains above reserve after corrective trial | Move volume to validated backup and pause delivery promises |
| Businesses insist on credit beyond available capital | Decline, require deposit, or finance transparently after separate analysis |
| Manual coordination exceeds tier economics | Reduce scope, raise fee, or automate the measured bottleneck |
| Founder spends all time on revisions and none on sales | Restrict custom options and hire priced design support |
